Fundamental needs

Fundamental needs are the essential expectations of the actors involved in a business. For TheGreenStay we identified five needs that the concept can satisfy — or damage.

Autonomy

The guest wants to decide what happens in the room. The concept gives that control, but only as long as joining stays voluntary and a choice can be reversed.

Comfort

The guest expects a room that is pleasant to stay in. Less cleaning must not mean the room becomes unpleasant or that supplies run out.

Hygiene

This is the need where the concept carries the most risk. A skipped clean must never lead to a room that does not meet the expectation.

Trust

The guest must be able to rely on the credits being correct and on the hotel's sustainability promise matching what actually happens.

Recognition

Whoever contributes wants that to be seen. The credits are tangible recognition, but the guest must also feel appreciated rather than feel that this is a cost saving for the hotel.

And the staff?

Housekeeping staff have needs of their own: predictable work, clear instructions and not the feeling that their hours are under pressure. That is a point that needs separate attention in the elaboration.

Why this is the tension at the heart of the concept

Autonomy, comfort and hygiene are in tension with the saving. The more cleans are skipped, the greater the environmental return — and the greater the risk that the guest feels less well cared for. The concept only works if the guest experiences gaining something rather than losing something.

This is the trade-off that returns in the co-destruction analysis: the needs cannot all be met to the maximum at the same time, and the point where something is given up is exactly where the concept can derail.

The concrete risks and the proposed measures are on the risk page.

Five needs, five greens

All five needs matter equally, but they do not all pull in the same direction. That is exactly why we named them separately instead of filing them under one heading of guest satisfaction.

A concept that steers only on saving gives way on hygiene and comfort. A concept that steers only on comfort gives nothing back. The skill lies in the choice the guest makes personally.
