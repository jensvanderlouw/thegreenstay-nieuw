TheGreenStay · Design challenge

Cleaning when you want it. Not when the schedule says so.

TheGreenStay gives hotel guests the option to skip housekeeping. The hotel saves water, energy and cleaning products — and shares part of that saving back with the guest in the form of credits.

See how it works What it delivers

Why this concept

In hotels, a room is cleaned every day by default, even when the guest never asks for it. That costs money and raw materials, and it gives the guest nothing extra in return. TheGreenStay turns that around: the choice moves to the guest and the return is shared.

Core

The guest decides

The guest decides per day whether the room is cleaned, and can change that choice at any moment.

Core

A saving becomes a reward

Part of what the hotel saves goes back to the guest as credits — for example to spend on a drink at the bar.

Core

Less consumption

Less laundry, less water, fewer cleaning products and less energy per stay.

Four principles that keep the concept standing

These came out of our co-destruction analysis: where the concept could destroy value, and what is needed to prevent that.

1. Cleaning stays available

Choosing less housekeeping must never feel like an obligation or like a lesser service.

2. Reception, housekeeping and bar share the same information

If the systems do not talk to each other, the guest gets an unclean room while the credits have already been deducted.

3. Credits follow the measured saving

The reward is set on what the hotel actually saves, not on an assumption.

4. Measure impact and listen

Consumption and guest feedback are tracked, so the concept can be adjusted when reality turns out different.

Where the concept comes from

The idea started with a simple observation: daily cleaning is a habit, not a request from the guest. Once you turn that habit into a choice, there is room to share the return instead of quietly keeping it.

Nature is not the decoration for this story, it is the argument itself. That is why the forest appears on this site.
